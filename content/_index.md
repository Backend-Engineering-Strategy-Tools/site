Started as a backend developer in 2007. Java, enterprise systems. Spent the last decade moving deeper into Kubernetes, cloud infrastructure, and platform engineering.

I apply software engineering discipline to infrastructure. I treat platforms as products that enable development teams, not just something to keep the lights on. I've done this across startup and enterprise contexts, including five years as sole infrastructure owner and COO at Synkzone. There I championed the move from VPS to Kubernetes and grew the platform from around 10 to 300+ running instances of a handful of core services. I learned what multi-cloud complexity actually costs you.

Most recently consulting through Accelerate@Iver practice, helping teams speed up cloud migrations and platform adoption.

At my best when given ownership of a hard problem. Greenfield platform builds, messy migrations, or getting an organization to actually adopt better practices.

Outside of work I'm a builder by nature. DIY, making things, figuring out how something works by taking it apart. I grew up with a large garage where my dad and I were always building something, that's where the instinct comes from. These days it shows up as homelab and garage, same instinct pointed at hardware instead of software. Currently teaching myself CAD and Blender for 3D printing, badly at first, less badly now, plus casting in metal on the side. Half my hobbies turned into the job at some point, which says something about the field, or about me.

Most of my time outside that goes to being a father. Family comes first, scout leader by night on top of that. When there's time left over I'm reading into whatever nerdy topic has my attention, science and sci-fi/fantasy mostly, though it's leaned more fact-based as I've gotten older.

This site is the professional side. [Experience](/experience/), learning in public with [notes](/public-notes/) that go deeper on the tools and practices, a [homelab](/homelab/) where I break things intentionally, and a [garage](/garage/) where I build them.

And ofc a few [projects](/projects/) worth sharing !
